# Bank Customer Churn

## Project Overview

The **Bank Customer Churn** project analyzes customer data to identify patterns and predict churn behavior. The dataset consists of information on 10,000 customers, including demographics, account details, and transaction history. This analysis uncovers key factors contributing to churn and provides actionable insights to improve customer retention strategies. The overall churn rate is **20.38%**, with significant churn rates observed among specific age groups, regions, and customer segments.

## Executive Summary

The analysis highlights key drivers of customer churn in the banking sector. Customers aged 30–40 exhibit the highest churn rates, while those with longer tenures are less likely to churn. Customers with low satisfaction scores**, single product purchases, or low credit scores are particularly at risk. Additionally, Germany has the highest churn rate of 32.44%, followed by Spain. Diamond cardholders also show elevated churn rates. These insights can guide targeted interventions to reduce churn and improve customer retention.

## Key Insights

### Demographics and Regional Analysis
- **Age Group**: Customers aged **30–40** have the highest churn rates.
- **Region**: **Germany** leads with the highest churn rate at **32.44%**, followed by **Spain**.
- **Tenure**: Long-tenure customers (over 8 years) have the lowest churn rates, while new customers (0–1 years) show the highest churn, especially among those with silver and diamond credit card types.

### Behavioral and Account Analysis
- **Card Type**: **Diamond cardholders** exhibit the highest churn rates.
- **Satisfaction Score**: Customers with a score of **2** churn the most, indicating dissatisfaction with services.
- **Products Purchased**: Customers with only **1 product** are more likely to churn.
- **Credit Score**: The highest churn rates are observed among customers with scores around **300**.
- **Points Earned**: Customers earning points in the range of **400–600** are the most likely to churn.
- **Complaints**: Customers who have submitted complaints are more likely to churn, even if they are active members.

## Recommendations

1. **Enhance Engagement with New Customers**  
   Focus on retention strategies for customers in their first year, offering loyalty rewards and proactive support.

2. **Improve Customer Satisfaction**  
   Address dissatisfaction among customers with low satisfaction scores by improving services and resolving complaints swiftly.

3. **Region-Specific Strategies**  
   Tailor retention strategies for high-churn regions like Germany and Spain with localized offerings and customer care initiatives.

4. **Encourage Multi-Product Adoption**  
   Promote cross-selling opportunities to increase engagement and reduce churn among single-product customers.

5. **Evaluate Membership and Card Programs**  
   Reassess benefits for silver and diamond memberships to meet customer expectations and enhance value.
